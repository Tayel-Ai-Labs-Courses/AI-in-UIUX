# References

Look at these as a designer — steal structure and reasoning, not screens.

## Products in this space

- **n8n** — what you are redesigning. Use it first.
- **Zapier** — the opposite philosophy: linear, no canvas, aggressively simple.
  Study what they gave up to get that.
- **Make (Integromat)** — a canvas that is beautiful and, for many users,
  unreadable. A good lesson in the cost of visual richness.
- **Retool / Airplane** — internal-tool builders. Look at their run logs.
- **Vercel / Netlify deploy logs** — the best build-failure UI anywhere. Note
  how they show a failed step without dumping a stack trace at you.
- **Stripe Dashboard** — the reference for showing status and money without
  panic. Look at how much white space it spends.

## Reading

- *Refactoring UI* — Wathan & Schoger. The most practical design book there is.
- *Designing Interfaces* — Tidwell. Patterns for exactly this kind of product.
- *Don't Make Me Think* — Krug. Short, still correct.
- Nielsen Norman Group, on error messages and on dashboard design.

## Systems worth reading

- Atlassian Design System
- Shopify Polaris — especially their writing guidelines
- IBM Carbon — dense data UI, done seriously
- Material 3 — read its reasoning, don't copy its look
